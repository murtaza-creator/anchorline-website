What Is Axminster Carpet? A Guide for Hotels and Corporate Interiors

By Anchorline  ·  September 2026  ·  5 min read

When a luxury hotel specifies carpet for its main lobby or a corporate boardroom requires flooring that will still look pristine after a decade of daily use, the answer is almost always the same: Axminster carpet. Yet despite its ubiquity in premium hospitality and corporate interiors, the name causes confusion — many designers and fit-out contractors aren't sure what makes Axminster different from standard broadloom, or why it commands a premium. This guide covers both.

Woven, Not Tufted — Why the Distinction Matters

Most carpet sold in India today is tufted. In tufted carpet, pile yarns are punched through a pre-woven backing fabric using a tufting gun — fast, economical, and versatile, but the pile is held in place by an adhesive secondary backing rather than by the structure of the weave itself.

Axminster carpet is woven. The pile yarns are interlocked into the backing structure during the weaving process itself, on a traditional Axminster loom. There is no secondary adhesive backing — the pile is mechanically secured by the weave. The practical consequences of this are significant:

  • Pile security: Because the pile is woven in rather than glued, individual tufts cannot work loose over time. This is why Axminster is the standard specification for high-footfall areas where tuft loss in a tufted carpet would be a maintenance problem.
  • Pattern definition: Axminster looms can weave complex, multi-colour patterns row by row with a precision that tufted manufacturing cannot match. Medallion motifs, custom border designs, and fine geometric repeats all reproduce cleanly.
  • Durability: The woven structure compresses and recovers differently from tufted pile. In heavy-traffic corridors and lobbies, an Axminster carpet will retain its pile height and visual integrity significantly longer than an equivalent-weight tufted product.
  • Dimensional stability: Woven construction has very low stretch, making it easier to install over large areas without the rippling or lifting that can affect tufted carpet over time.

"In premium hospitality, Axminster isn't specified for its looks alone — it's specified because it performs. A lobby carpet needs to look the same in year three as it did on opening day."

The 80/20 Wool-Nylon Blend

Traditional Axminster carpet is made from an 80% wool / 20% nylon blend — a formulation that has been standard in the hospitality industry for decades. The wool component provides natural resilience, warmth underfoot, and a surface that responds beautifully to light. The nylon component adds abrasion resistance and tensile strength, extending service life in high-traffic areas.

This blend is specifically engineered for commercial hospitality use. Pure wool Axminster exists for residential applications, but the 80/20 blend is the hospitality standard — it is what specifiers mean when they write "Axminster, hospitality grade" on a schedule of finishes.

Where Axminster Is Specified

Axminster is not a general-purpose carpet. It is a premium specification for spaces where both visual impact and long-term performance are non-negotiable:

  • Hotel lobbies and reception areas — the single highest-visibility surface in any hotel, where footfall is constant and first impressions are permanent
  • Banquet halls and ballrooms — where large-format pattern repeats can be accommodated and the scale of the space justifies the investment
  • VIP suites and premium guest corridors — where the tactile quality of wool pile is part of the guest experience
  • Corporate boardrooms and executive floors — where a woven carpet signals permanence and considered specification rather than off-the-shelf fit-out
  • Heritage and boutique hotels — where traditional woven construction is consistent with a narrative of craftsmanship and authenticity

Why Axminster Over Standard Broadloom?

Standard broadloom carpet — tufted, typically in a 100% nylon or polypropylene construction — is entirely appropriate for standard guest rooms, back-of-house corridors, and budget hospitality projects. It is cost-effective, quick to install, and available in a wide range of weights and constructions.

The case for Axminster in premium areas comes down to three things:

  • Lifecycle cost: A higher-quality woven carpet specified correctly will outlast two or three replacement cycles of a cheaper tufted product. Over a 10-year hotel ownership period, the total cost of ownership often favours the Axminster specification.
  • Custom design: Axminster can be woven to a project-specific pattern, colourway, and border configuration. Standard broadloom is selected from existing ranges. For a hotel with a defined design identity, the ability to have a bespoke floor pattern is a meaningful differentiator.
  • Material integrity: The wool-blend surface has a visual and tactile quality that synthetic broadloom cannot replicate — particularly under the warm lighting common in hospitality environments. This matters to guests, even those who cannot articulate why the floor feels right.

Custom Design and Project Specification

One of the most underused capabilities of Axminster is the design flexibility it offers. Unlike standard broadloom where the designer selects from an existing collection, Axminster can be woven to a fully bespoke design — a hotel's own medallion pattern, a custom colourway that coordinates with FF&E specifications, a border detail that frames a corridor or pre-function space.

The design process typically works as follows: the designer provides artwork (or works with the supplier to develop it), colourways are confirmed from yarn samples, and the carpet is woven to exact room dimensions. Lead times for bespoke Axminster are longer than stock broadloom — typically 12 to 20 weeks from artwork approval — but the result is a floor that is entirely specific to the project.
